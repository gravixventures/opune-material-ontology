# OPUNÉ® Evaluation, Testing & Validation Taxonomy

## Purpose

This document defines the evaluation vocabulary used within the OPUNÉ® Material Ontology.

Evaluation describes the methods, conditions, measurements, comparisons, specifications, and validation activities used to understand a defined material system.

Evaluation is distinct from a material claim.

The core relationship is:

`Material System → Application → Requirement → Specification → Evaluation → Evidence → Validation`

A material should not be considered suitable for a specific application solely because it has undergone general characterization.

---

## Evaluation Classes

The current evaluation taxonomy includes:

1. Material Characterization
2. Physical Evaluation
3. Mechanical Evaluation
4. Surface Evaluation
5. Flexibility Evaluation
6. Durability Evaluation
7. Dimensional Evaluation
8. Processing Evaluation
9. Application-Specific Testing
10. Laboratory Testing
11. Comparative Evaluation
12. Lifecycle Assessment
13. Regulatory and Compliance Evaluation

These categories may overlap within a single development program.

---

## Material Characterization

Material characterization describes the identification and measurement of relevant material properties or characteristics.

Potential characterization areas include:

- Composition
- Structure
- Morphology
- Density
- Thickness
- Moisture characteristics
- Surface characteristics
- Fiber characteristics
- Particle characteristics
- Dimensional characteristics

Characterization should identify the relevant material configuration and measurement method.

A characterization result does not automatically establish application suitability.

---

## Physical Evaluation

Physical evaluation examines measurable physical characteristics of a defined material system.

Potential parameters include:

- Thickness
- Mass
- Density
- Dimensional characteristics
- Surface characteristics
- Moisture-related characteristics
- Structural characteristics

The selected parameters depend on the material architecture and intended application.

Values should be reported only with sufficient information regarding:

- Material configuration
- Test or measurement method
- Specimen condition
- Units
- Relevant environmental conditions

---

## Mechanical Evaluation

Mechanical evaluation examines the response of a material system to defined mechanical loading or deformation conditions.

Potential evaluation categories include:

- Tensile behaviour
- Tear behaviour
- Compression behaviour
- Flexural behaviour
- Elongation
- Strength
- Stiffness
- Other application-specific mechanical characteristics

Mechanical results are dependent on:

- Material construction
- Specimen configuration
- Test method
- Test conditions
- Direction of measurement
- Environmental conditions

A mechanical result from one material architecture should not automatically be generalized to another.

---

## Surface Evaluation

Surface evaluation examines characteristics associated with the exposed surface of a material system.

Potential evaluation categories include:

- Surface morphology
- Surface texture
- Surface finish
- Surface uniformity
- Abrasion
- Adhesion
- Coating behaviour
- Surface durability
- Visual characteristics

Surface evaluation may be particularly relevant to applications where tactile, visual, wear, or finishing requirements are important.

---

## Flexibility Evaluation

Flexibility evaluation examines how a defined material system responds to bending, folding, deformation, or related movement.

Potential evaluation considerations include:

- Bending behaviour
- Flexural response
- Fold behaviour
- Recovery
- Crack formation
- Repeated flexing
- Construction-specific flexibility

Flexibility is dependent on:

- Material architecture
- Thickness
- Layer configuration
- Substrate
- Finish
- Test method
- Application construction

A material should not be described as universally flexible without defined evidence.

---

## Durability Evaluation

Durability evaluation examines material behaviour under defined exposure, loading, or repeated-use conditions.

Potential categories include:

- Abrasion
- Repeated flexing
- Tear resistance
- Surface wear
- Environmental exposure
- Moisture exposure
- Cleaning exposure
- Dimensional stability
- Aging-related behaviour

Durability is application-specific.

A durability result obtained under one test condition does not establish service life under unrelated conditions.

---

## Dimensional Evaluation

Dimensional evaluation examines the dimensional behaviour of a material system.

Potential parameters include:

- Thickness
- Length
- Width
- Dimensional stability
- Shrinkage
- Expansion
- Warpage
- Geometry retention

Measurements should identify the relevant specimen condition and method.

---

## Processing Evaluation

Processing evaluation examines how a material behaves during manufacturing or conversion.

Potential processing operations include:

- Cutting
- Stitching
- Bonding
- Lamination
- Forming
- Molding
- Pressing
- Finishing
- Surface treatment
- Conversion

Processing evaluation may consider:

- Formability
- Handling
- Bonding compatibility
- Lamination behaviour
- Surface response
- Edge behaviour
- Manufacturing consistency

Processing suitability must be evaluated against the actual manufacturing process.

---

## Application-Specific Testing

Application-specific testing evaluates a material system against defined requirements of an intended application.

The relationship is:

`Application → Requirement → Test Method → Result → Assessment`

Examples of application contexts include:

- Automotive interiors
- Footwear
- Fashion
- Leather goods
- Furniture
- Architectural interiors
- Packaging
- Aviation
- Industrial products

Application-specific testing should not be replaced by generic material characterization.

---

## Laboratory Testing

Laboratory testing refers to evaluation conducted under defined laboratory conditions using an identified method or procedure.

A laboratory result should, where applicable, identify:

- Test method
- Standard or procedure
- Specimen configuration
- Material construction
- Test conditions
- Units
- Measurement result
- Laboratory or testing source
- Relevant date or version

A laboratory result does not automatically establish regulatory compliance or commercial qualification.

---

## Comparative Evaluation

Comparative evaluation examines two or more defined material systems or reference materials under comparable conditions.

A meaningful comparison requires:

- Defined materials
- Comparable specimen configurations
- Defined test methods
- Comparable conditions
- Consistent measurement units
- Appropriate interpretation

Comparative results should not be generalized beyond the tested configurations.

---

## Technical Specification

A technical specification defines measurable or otherwise verifiable requirements for a material system.

A specification may contain:

- Parameter
- Requirement
- Unit
- Test method
- Acceptance criterion
- Material configuration
- Application context

A specification should be distinguished from an observed result.

The relationship is:

`Requirement → Specification → Test → Result → Assessment`

A target specification is not evidence of achieved performance.

---

## Test Method

A test method defines how an evaluation is conducted.

A test method may identify:

- Procedure
- Equipment
- Specimen configuration
- Conditioning
- Environmental conditions
- Loading or exposure
- Measurement
- Calculation
- Reporting requirements

Where a recognized standard is used, the applicable standard identification and version should be documented.

---

## Test Result

A test result is an observed or calculated output generated from a defined evaluation.

A test result should not be detached from its:

- Material
- Architecture
- Specimen
- Method
- Conditions
- Units
- Context

The preferred representation is:

`Material System + Test Method + Conditions + Result`

rather than:

`Material + Number`

---

## Validation

Validation examines whether a defined material system satisfies defined requirements for a specified purpose.

The relationship is:

`Requirement → Test / Evaluation → Evidence → Assessment → Validation Decision`

Validation may be:

- Material-level
- Process-level
- Application-level
- Specification-level
- Regulatory-level

The scope of validation must be explicitly stated.

Validation for one application should not automatically be interpreted as validation for another.

---

## Qualification

Qualification is a defined process through which a material, component, supplier, process, or system is assessed against specified requirements for a particular use.

Qualification may require:

- Defined specifications
- Defined testing
- Repeated evaluation
- Manufacturing assessment
- Regulatory assessment
- Application trials
- Customer or OEM requirements

Qualification status should only be stated when supported by appropriate documentation.

---

## Regulatory and Compliance Evaluation

Regulatory and compliance evaluation examines whether a defined material system meets applicable legal, regulatory, contractual, or certification requirements.

Relevant requirements depend on:

- Jurisdiction
- Application
- Product category
- Material composition
- Intended use
- Manufacturing process

Regulatory compliance should never be inferred from general sustainability, plant-based, biomaterial, or laboratory-testing terminology.

---

## Lifecycle Assessment

Lifecycle assessment evaluates environmental impacts associated with a defined product system and defined system boundary using a specified methodology.

Potential lifecycle stages include:

- Feedstock sourcing
- Feedstock preparation
- Material processing
- Conversion
- Distribution
- Use
- End-of-life

Lifecycle assessment requires defined:

- Functional unit
- System boundary
- Inventory data
- Allocation rules where applicable
- Impact assessment method
- Assumptions
- Data sources
- Interpretation

A lifecycle statement should not be presented as a universal property of a feedstock or material category without a defined assessment.

---

## Three-Stage Lifecycle Evaluation

Where a three-stage lifecycle framework is used, the stages should be explicitly defined.

A general structure may be:

`Stage 1 → Feedstock and Material Inputs`

`Stage 2 → Processing and Material Development`

`Stage 3 → Use and End-of-Life Considerations`

The precise boundaries, datasets, assumptions, and calculation methodology must be documented for the specific assessment.

The existence of a three-stage framework does not itself constitute a completed lifecycle assessment.

---

## Evidence Classification

Evaluation evidence should be classified according to its source and status.

### Primary Test Data

Data generated through direct testing or measurement of a defined material system.

### Laboratory Report

A documented report containing test results and relevant testing information.

### Published Evidence

Scientific or technical evidence available through published literature or authoritative technical sources.

### Derived Result

A value calculated from documented primary or published data.

### Specification

A defined requirement against which a material may be evaluated.

### Target

A future objective that has not yet been demonstrated.

### Hypothesis

A proposition requiring investigation.

### Validation Result

A documented assessment establishing whether a defined requirement has been satisfied within a defined scope.

---

## Evidence Quality

Evaluation evidence should be interpreted according to:

1. Source
2. Method
3. Material definition
4. Test conditions
5. Data quality
6. Reproducibility where applicable
7. Scope of applicability

A numerical result without sufficient context should not be treated as a complete technical claim.
