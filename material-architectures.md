# OPUNÉ® Material Architecture Taxonomy

## Purpose

This document defines the material architecture vocabulary used within the OPUNÉ® Material Ontology.

Material architecture describes how plant-derived feedstocks, intermediate materials, and other components are organized, processed, combined, or configured into a material system.

Material architecture is distinct from feedstock identity.

The same feedstock may support different architectures, while different feedstocks may be incorporated into similar architectural classes.

## Core Relationship

The primary relationship is:

`Feedstock → Processing → Material Architecture → Material System → Application → Evaluation`

Material architecture therefore represents an intermediate technical layer between feedstock and application.

## Architecture Classes

The current ontology includes the following architecture classes:

1. Fibrous Structures
2. Non-Woven Architectures
3. Particulate Composites
4. Molded Forms
5. Rigid Matrices
6. Flexible Bio-Layers
7. Composite Substrates
8. Surface-Layer Systems
9. Backing Architectures

These classes are descriptive and may overlap within a single material system.

---

## Fibrous Structures

A fibrous structure is a material architecture in which plant-derived fibers or fiber-like constituents form a significant structural component.

Relevant characteristics may include:

- Fiber morphology
- Fiber dimensions
- Fiber orientation
- Fiber distribution
- Fiber bonding
- Fiber density
- Moisture characteristics
- Processing behaviour

Potential development pathways include:

`Fibrous Feedstock → Fiber Preparation → Fiber Network → Consolidation → Material System`

Examples of feedstocks that may be investigated for fibrous architectures include:

- Wheat straw
- Rice straw
- Corn stalk
- Corn husk
- Cotton stalk
- Banana pseudostem
- Coconut coir

The presence of a fibrous architecture does not establish a specific performance level.

---

## Non-Woven Architectures

A non-woven architecture is a structure formed from fibers or other elements assembled into a coherent sheet, web, or related configuration without relying on conventional woven construction.

Relevant development parameters may include:

- Fiber characteristics
- Fiber distribution
- Web structure
- Orientation
- Bonding mechanism
- Consolidation
- Thickness
- Surface structure
- Processing conditions

Potential relationship:

`Fibrous Feedstock → Fiber Preparation → Web Formation → Bonding / Consolidation → Non-Woven Material`

Material suitability depends on the resulting construction and defined application requirements.

---

## Particulate Composites

A particulate composite architecture incorporates particulate material within a matrix or other continuous phase.

Relevant parameters may include:

- Particle characteristics
- Particle size
- Particle distribution
- Particle loading
- Surface characteristics
- Matrix characteristics
- Interfacial behaviour
- Processing conditions
- Consolidation

Potential relationship:

`Particulate Feedstock → Particle Preparation → Formulation → Consolidation → Particulate Composite`

Feedstocks that may be investigated within this architecture include:

- Rice husk
- Coconut shell
- Groundnut shell

The feedstock itself does not determine the properties of the resulting composite.

---

## Molded Forms

A molded form is a material architecture produced through a forming process that establishes a defined three-dimensional geometry.

Relevant parameters may include:

- Feedstock preparation
- Formulation
- Mold geometry
- Forming conditions
- Consolidation
- Drying or curing
- Surface characteristics
- Dimensional stability

Potential relationship:

`Feedstock → Preparation → Formulation → Molding → Consolidation → Molded Material`

Molded architecture may incorporate fibrous, particulate, or composite constituents.

---

## Rigid Matrices

A rigid matrix architecture describes a material configuration in which a continuous matrix provides structural organization around or between other constituents.

Relevant parameters may include:

- Matrix composition
- Reinforcement characteristics
- Filler characteristics
- Interfacial bonding
- Consolidation
- Geometry
- Processing conditions
- Structural requirements

Potential relationship:

`Feedstock → Reinforcement / Particulate Preparation → Matrix Integration → Consolidation → Rigid Material System`

Rigid classification should be based on the defined material configuration and its observed behaviour rather than feedstock identity alone.

---

## Flexible Bio-Layers

A flexible bio-layer is a thin or relatively flexible material layer incorporated into a material system.

Relevant parameters may include:

- Layer composition
- Thickness
- Flexibility
- Surface structure
- Substrate interaction
- Bonding
- Finishing
- Bending behaviour
- Application-specific durability

Potential relationship:

`Plant-Derived Input → Material Formation → Layer Architecture → Surface / Backing Integration → Material System`

A flexible architecture does not imply a particular mechanical performance without defined testing.

---

## Composite Substrates

A composite substrate is a supporting material architecture containing multiple constituents or layers that provide a defined carrier or structural base.

Relevant parameters may include:

- Substrate composition
- Fiber or particulate structure
- Layer configuration
- Thickness
- Flexibility
- Surface compatibility
- Bonding
- Processing behaviour

Potential relationship:

`Feedstock → Structural Layer → Composite Integration → Substrate → Surface System`

Composite substrates may subsequently receive coatings, finishes, surface layers, or other components.

---

## Surface-Layer Systems

A surface-layer system describes a material architecture in which a defined surface layer contributes to the material's external interface.

Relevant parameters may include:

- Surface composition
- Surface texture
- Surface morphology
- Finish
- Coating
- Layer thickness
- Adhesion
- Abrasion requirements
- Application requirements

Potential relationship:

`Base Material → Surface Layer → Finishing → Defined Material System`

Surface characteristics must be evaluated in relation to the complete material construction.

---

## Backing Architectures

A backing architecture provides structural, dimensional, processing, or handling support behind another material layer.

Relevant parameters may include:

- Backing composition
- Fiber structure
- Flexibility
- Thickness
- Dimensional behaviour
- Surface compatibility
- Adhesion
- Lamination behaviour
- Application requirements

Potential relationship:

`Backing → Surface Layer → Lamination / Bonding → Composite Material System`

A backing is part of the material system and should not automatically be interpreted as part of the plant-derived fraction.

---

## Multi-Layer Architectures

A material system may combine multiple architecture classes.

For example:

`Fibrous Structure + Composite Substrate + Surface Layer`

or:

`Particulate Composite + Rigid Matrix + Surface Finish`

or:

`Non-Woven Architecture + Backing + Surface Layer`

The ontology therefore permits multiple architecture relationships within one material system.

## Architecture Components

A material architecture may contain:

### Primary Structural Component

The component providing a principal structural function.

### Matrix

A continuous phase that surrounds, binds, or organizes other constituents where applicable.

### Reinforcement

A component incorporated to influence structural behaviour.

### Filler

A particulate or other constituent incorporated into a matrix or material structure.

### Substrate

A supporting layer or structural carrier.

### Backing

A supporting layer positioned behind another material layer.

### Surface Layer

A layer forming or contributing to the external surface.

### Coating

A deposited or applied layer used to modify defined surface or material characteristics.

### Finish

A surface treatment or finishing configuration applied during material development or conversion.

The presence and function of these components must be documented for the specific material system.

---

## Architecture Attributes

A material architecture may be described using common attributes.

### Geometry

Potential descriptors include:

- Sheet
- Layer
- Web
- Panel
- Molded form
- Composite structure
- Three-dimensional form

### Structure

Potential descriptors include:

- Fibrous
- Particulate
- Composite
- Multi-layer
- Surface-layer
- Substrate-supported
- Matrix-based

### Physical Configuration

Potential descriptors include:

- Flexible
- Semi-rigid
- Rigid
- Application-dependent

These descriptors should be assigned based on documented material characteristics rather than assumed from feedstock identity.

### Surface

Potential descriptors include:

- Texture
- Finish
- Surface morphology
- Coating
- Surface treatment

Specific values require supporting technical documentation.

---

## Architecture and Processing

Processing is directly connected to architecture.

A simplified relationship is:

`Feedstock → Preparation → Formulation → Forming → Consolidation → Architecture`

Different processing pathways may produce different architectures from the same feedstock.

Therefore:

`One Feedstock ≠ One Material`

and:

`One Feedstock ≠ One Application`

The resulting material system must be evaluated independently.

---

## Architecture and Application

Material architecture should be connected to an application only through a defined material system and appropriate evaluation.

Preferred relationship:

`Material Architecture → Material System → Application → Evaluation`

An architecture class alone does not establish suitability for:

- Automotive interiors
- Footwear
- Fashion
- Leather goods
- Furniture
- Architectural interiors
- Packaging
- Aviation
- Other industrial applications

Application suitability is specification-dependent.

---

## Architecture and Evaluation

Evaluation should correspond to the architecture and intended application.

Potential evaluation categories include:

- Physical characterization
- Mechanical testing
- Flexibility evaluation
- Surface evaluation
- Dimensional evaluation
- Durability assessment
- Processing evaluation
- Application-specific testing
- Laboratory testing

Test results should identify the relevant material construction and test conditions.

A test result for one architecture should not automatically be generalized to another architecture.

---

## Architecture and Lifecycle Evaluation

Lifecycle evaluation may depend on architecture-specific factors including:

- Feedstock preparation
- Material processing
- Formulation
- Component composition
- Energy use
- Water use
- Conversion
- Surface treatment
- Coating
- Use
- End-of-life pathway

Lifecycle conclusions require defined system boundaries, methodologies, datasets, assumptions, and evidence.

---

## Evidence Classification

Architecture information should be classified according to its evidence source.

### Primary Material Data

Data generated through direct material development, characterization, processing, or testing.

### Published Evidence

Information supported by scientific or technical literature.

### Derived Information

Information calculated or interpreted from documented data.

### Development Hypothesis

A proposed architecture or processing pathway requiring investigation.

### Application Requirement

A requirement defined by an industrial application or customer specification.

### Target

A future material or performance objective that has not yet been demonstrated.

---

## Data Discipline

The following should not be assigned to an architecture without supporting evidence:

- Universal mechanical properties
- Universal durability
- Universal flexibility values
- Universal thickness ranges
- Universal density values
- Universal environmental performance
- Universal biodegradability
- Universal application suitability
- Universal processing conditions

Architecture terminology describes structure; it does not substitute for measurement.

---

## Architecture Relationship Map

The core ontology relationships are:

`Feedstock → Processing`

`Processing → Material Architecture`

`Material Architecture → Material System`

`Material System → Application`

`Application → Evaluation`

`Feedstock → Lifecycle Evaluation`

`Processing → Lifecycle Evaluation`

`Material Architecture → Lifecycle Evaluation`

`Evaluation → Technical Specification`

## Current Architecture Vocabulary

| Architecture | Structural Context |
|---|---|
| Fibrous Structures | Fiber-based material structures |
| Non-Woven Architectures | Fiber or web structures formed without conventional weaving |
| Particulate Composites | Particulate constituents incorporated into a matrix or continuous phase |
| Molded Forms | Three-dimensional structures produced through forming or molding |
| Rigid Matrices | Matrix-based structures intended for defined structural configurations |
| Flexible Bio-Layers | Flexible plant-derived or plant-containing material layers |
| Composite Substrates | Multi-constituent supporting material structures |
| Surface-Layer Systems | Material systems incorporating a defined surface layer |
| Backing Architectures | Supporting layers positioned behind another material layer |

This vocabulary describes architecture classes. It does not represent a universal industry classification.

---

## Status

This material architecture taxonomy is an evolving component of the OPUNÉ® Material Ontology.

New architecture classes should be introduced only when they represent a meaningful technical distinction supported by documented material-development, scientific, processing, application, or evaluation evidence.

All substantive changes should be version-controlled.
