# OPUNÉ® Controlled Technical Terminology

## Purpose

This document defines the controlled terminology used within the OPUNÉ® Material Ontology.

The purpose is to maintain consistent meaning across technical documentation, material dossiers, research resources, application descriptions, evaluation records, and machine-readable data.

A term should retain the same technical meaning wherever it is used within the ontology.

---

## Biomaterial

A material system incorporating biological or plant-derived inputs.

The term does not by itself establish:

- Environmental benefit
- Biodegradability
- Compostability
- Regulatory classification
- Performance
- Application suitability

---

## Plant-Based Biomaterial

A biomaterial system containing plant-derived inputs.

The term does not by itself establish that every constituent of the complete material system is plant-derived.

---

## Plant-Derived Feedstock

A biological material originating from a plant source and investigated or used as an input for material development.

---

## Agricultural Residue

Plant-derived material arising from agricultural production or associated agricultural processing.

Agricultural residue characteristics depend on the crop, production system, processing pathway, geography, and collection conditions.

---

## Agro-Industrial Residue

A material generated as a residual stream from agricultural or agro-industrial processing activities.

---

## Feedstock

A biological, plant-derived, agricultural-residue, or other defined input investigated or used as a starting material for biomaterial development.

Feedstock is not synonymous with finished material.

---

## Intermediate

A material or processed form that exists between the original feedstock and the final material system.

An intermediate may result from:

- Preparation
- Separation
- Size reduction
- Fiber preparation
- Particle preparation
- Drying
- Formulation
- Other processing

---

## Material Architecture

The structural organization of constituents within a material system.

Examples include:

- Fibrous structures
- Non-woven architectures
- Particulate composites
- Molded forms
- Rigid matrices
- Flexible bio-layers
- Composite substrates
- Surface-layer systems
- Backing architectures

---

## Material System

The complete material configuration being evaluated for a defined purpose or application.

A material system may contain multiple components or layers.

---

## Material Component

An individual constituent of a material system.

Potential components include:

- Feedstock-derived material
- Matrix
- Reinforcement
- Filler
- Substrate
- Backing
- Surface layer
- Coating
- Finish

---

## Matrix

A continuous phase that surrounds, binds, or organizes other constituents within a material system.

Matrix identity and function should be documented for the specific material system.

---

## Reinforcement

A constituent incorporated into a material system to contribute to defined structural or functional behaviour.

Reinforcement should not be assumed to improve performance without supporting evidence.

---

## Filler

A particulate or other constituent incorporated into a matrix or material structure.

Filler classification does not itself establish a specific functional role.

---

## Substrate

A supporting material layer or structure onto which another material, layer, or component may be incorporated.

---

## Backing

A supporting layer positioned behind another material layer.

A backing may contribute to:

- Structure
- Flexibility
- Handling
- Dimensional behaviour
- Processing compatibility

---

## Surface Layer

A defined material layer contributing to the external surface of a material system.

---

## Coating

A material layer applied to a surface for a defined functional, protective, processing, or aesthetic purpose.

A coating is a separate component of the complete material system unless documented otherwise.

---

## Finish

A surface treatment or finishing configuration applied during material development or conversion.

Finish characteristics should be distinguished from intrinsic properties of the underlying material.

---

## Processing

The sequence of operations through which feedstock or intermediate materials are prepared, transformed, combined, formed, consolidated, finished, or converted.

---

## Feedstock Preparation

Operations performed to prepare a feedstock for downstream material development.

Potential activities include:

- Cleaning
- Sorting
- Conditioning
- Separation
- Moisture adjustment

---

## Size Reduction

Processing used to reduce the physical dimensions of a feedstock or intermediate.

Potential methods include:

- Cutting
- Chopping
- Milling
- Grinding
- Shredding

---

## Separation

Processing used to isolate defined fractions or constituents from a feedstock or intermediate.

---

## Fiber Preparation

Processing used to prepare plant-derived fibers for subsequent material development.

---

## Particle Preparation

Processing used to prepare particulate feedstocks or intermediate materials for material development.

---

## Formulation

The defined composition of a material system before or during forming, consolidation, or other downstream processing.

---

## Mixing

A process used to combine two or more constituents and establish a defined distribution within the mixture.

---

## Forming

Processing used to establish a defined geometry or material structure.

---

## Molding

A forming process used to establish a defined three-dimensional geometry.

---

## Consolidation

A process used to establish structural coherence within a material architecture.

---

## Lamination

A process used to join two or more material layers into a combined structure.

---

## Surface Treatment

A process used to modify the characteristics of a material surface.

---

## Conversion

Processing used to transform a developed material into a form suitable for a defined product or application.

---

## Application

The industrial context in which a defined material system is intended to be evaluated or used.

Application classification does not establish suitability.

---

## Application Requirement

A defined requirement associated with a particular application or use context.

---

## Material Specification

A defined set of measurable or verifiable requirements for a material system.

A specification is distinct from an observed result.

---

## Test Method

A defined procedure used to measure, characterize, compare, or assess a material system.

A test method defines how an evaluation is conducted.

---

## Test Result

An observed or calculated output produced by a defined test or evaluation.

A test result should remain associated with its:

- Material
- Method
- Conditions
- Units
- Scope

---

## Evaluation

A structured process used to characterize or assess a material system against defined parameters or requirements.

---

## Characterization

The measurement or documentation of defined material characteristics.

Characterization does not automatically establish application suitability.

---

## Validation

An assessment establishing whether defined requirements have been satisfied within a defined scope.

Validation is bounded by:

- Material
- Application
- Requirement
- Method
- Evidence
- Conditions

---

## Qualification

A defined assessment process used to establish suitability of a material, process, component, supplier, or system for a specified purpose.

Qualification status requires supporting documentation.

---

## Lifecycle Assessment

A defined methodology for evaluating environmental impacts associated with a product system across specified lifecycle stages and system boundaries.

Lifecycle conclusions require documented methodology, data, assumptions, and scope.

---

## Lifecycle Data

Data used to represent defined inputs, outputs, processes, or impacts within a lifecycle assessment.

---

## Functional Unit

A quantified reference unit used to provide a basis for comparison within a lifecycle assessment.

The functional unit must be explicitly defined for the assessment.

---

## System Boundary

The defined set of processes, stages, inputs, outputs, and activities included within an assessment.

---

## Primary Data

Data generated directly through measurement, testing, observation, processing, or documented operational activity.

---

## Published Evidence

Scientific or technical information supported by publicly available literature or authoritative technical publications.

---

## Derived Information

Information calculated, transformed, aggregated, or interpreted from documented source data.

---

## Comparative Evidence

Evidence generated through comparison of defined materials, processes, architectures, or systems under comparable conditions.

---

## Specification

A defined requirement or criterion used to evaluate a material or system.

A specification is not equivalent to a measured result.

---

## Target

A future objective that has not yet been demonstrated.

A target must remain distinct from an achieved result.

---

## Hypothesis

A proposition requiring investigation or validation.

A hypothesis must not be represented as an established technical fact without sufficient evidence.

---

## Evidence

Information supporting a technical claim, measurement, relationship, assessment, or status.

Evidence should retain its source and scope.

---

## Evidence Scope

The defined boundary within which evidence is applicable.

Scope may include:

- Material
- Feedstock
- Architecture
- Process
- Application
- Method
- Geography
- Time period
- Manufacturing scale

Evidence should not be generalized beyond its documented scope.

---

## Claim

A technical statement about a material, process, application, lifecycle, or other defined subject.

Claims should be connected to appropriate supporting evidence.

---

## Assessment

An interpretation of evidence against a defined requirement, question, or evaluation objective.

---

## Status

A defined state assigned to a material, process, evaluation, or evidence record.

Potential statuses include:

- Not Evaluated
- Under Evaluation
- Characterized
- Tested
- Validated
- Qualified
- Commercial Application

Status assignment requires appropriate supporting evidence.

---

## Commercial Application

A documented use of a defined material system within an actual commercial product or application.

Commercial application should not be inferred solely from laboratory development or proposed application pathways.

---

## Technical Data

Documented information describing material, process, testing, application, lifecycle, or related technical characteristics.

---

## Technical Data Sheet

A structured technical document containing defined information about a material system.

A technical data sheet should distinguish measured information from specifications, targets, and other descriptive information.

---

## Material Development

The investigation and engineering of feedstocks, formulations, processing pathways, architectures, and material systems toward defined requirements.

Material development may include experimental, laboratory, pilot, or production-scale activities.

---

## Material Evaluation

The structured assessment of a defined material system using specified parameters, methods, or requirements.

---

## Application-Specific Validation

Validation conducted against requirements defined for a particular application, component, product, or construction.

Application-specific validation should not automatically be generalized to unrelated applications.

---

## Technical Specification Relationship

The preferred terminology relationship is:

`Application Requirement → Material Specification → Test Method → Test Result → Evidence → Assessment → Validation`

This relationship separates requirements from observed performance.

---

## Material Development Relationship

The preferred material-development relationship is:

`Feedstock → Processing → Material Architecture → Material System → Application → Evaluation`

This relationship does not imply universal suitability.

---

## Lifecycle Relationship

The preferred lifecycle relationship is:

`Feedstock → Processing → Material System → Lifecycle Assessment`

Lifecycle assessment may also include application, conversion, use, and end-of-life stages depending on the defined system boundary.

---

## Terminology Discipline

The following distinctions must be preserved:

`Feedstock ≠ Material`

`Material Architecture ≠ Material System`

`Application ≠ Application Suitability`

`Requirement ≠ Result`

`Specification ≠ Result`

`Tested ≠ Validated`

`Validated ≠ Certified`

`Target ≠ Achieved Result`

`Hypothesis ≠ Established Fact`

`Plant-Based ≠ Automatically Sustainable`

`Plant-Derived ≠ Entirely Plant-Based`

`Laboratory Testing ≠ Commercial Qualification`

`Lifecycle Data ≠ Lifecycle Assessment`

These distinctions are fundamental to the ontology.

---

## Prohibited Semantic Shortcuts

The following relationships should not be created without supporting evidence:

`Plant-Based → Sustainable`

`Agricultural Residue → Lower Environmental Impact`

`Biomaterial → Biodegradable`

`Plant-Derived → Fully Plant-Based`

`Laboratory Tested → Commercially Qualified`

`Tested → Validated`

`Validated → Certified`

`Certified → Universally Compliant`

`Feedstock → Application Suitability`

These concepts require independent evidence and defined scope.

---

## Naming Principles

Technical terminology should be:

- Specific
- Consistent
- Descriptive
- Scope-aware
- Evidence-compatible

Terminology should not be selected solely for search-engine optimization or promotional effect.

---

## Versioning

Changes to controlled terminology should be version-controlled.

Substantive terminology changes should identify:

- Version
- Date
- Changed term
- Previous definition
- New definition
- Reason for change

---

## Status

This controlled terminology is an evolving component of the OPUNÉ® Material Ontology.

New terms should be introduced only when they represent a meaningful technical concept supported by material-development, scientific, processing, application, testing, regulatory, or lifecycle evidence.

All substantive changes should be version-controlled.
